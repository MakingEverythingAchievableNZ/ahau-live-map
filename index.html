What happens when you tap something

Behind the scenes of Āhau ~36 little building blocks team up to make it work. Let's use the example of opening someone's whakapapa (family tree). Click any step to jump to that piece further down the page.

  1. You tap something

    The Āhau screen on your phone or computer asks a question, like "show me this person's whakapapa."

  2. The request goes out

    Apollo Client packages it up and sends it to the app's own local server.

  3. The local server answers

    A small GraphQL server running on your own device receives the question.

  4. It's handed to a specialist

    The server routes "whakapapa" questions to the translator piece that knows how to ask for family-tree data specifically.

  5. The record-keeper does the work

    The piece that actually knows what a family relationship is looks up (or writes) the records.

  6. It lands in your own log

    The change is saved to your device's own private, permanent record.

What about the part where you sync your data with others? Nothing above waits for the internet. Once it's saved locally, it reaches other whanau members' devices later, peer-to-peer, whenever those devices next connect — over the internet, or just local wifi in the same room. If the record is marked private, that check happens on every single read and write, enforced by ssb-recps-guard.
