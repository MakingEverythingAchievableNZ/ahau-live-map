This is the piece that actually stores and manages family relationships — parent-child links, partnerships, and the different kinds of connection that can exist between two people, including a birth relationship, a legal adoption, or whāngai (the practice of raising a child within the wider whānau). Each relationship is kept as its own record with its own history, so it can be corrected or marked as no longer current without affecting anyone else's information.

It also manages "views" — a saved way of looking at a family tree from one person's perspective, such as showing everyone descended from a particular ancestor. Because family trees can get tangled (for example, when two people are related to each other in more than one way through intermarriage), a view can also remember which relationship should be shown as the "main" one for a person, so the diagram stays readable.
